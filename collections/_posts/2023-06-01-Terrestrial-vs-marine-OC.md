In our recent study published in *Geophysical Research Letters* (see here [10.1029/2025GL117348](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2025GL117348)), we investigate how organic carbon is transported, mixed, and preserved at the mouth of the Columbia River. This margin is highly energetic, shaped by strong waves, tides, and seasonal river discharge, making it an ideal setting to examine how terrestrial carbon from the watershed interacts with marine-derived organic matter produced on the shelf. Rather than behaving as a steady depositional system, we find that carbon delivery and redistribution occur in pulses tied to high-discharge and storm events. These episodic forcings control when and where organic carbon is buried, resuspended, or exported offshore.
