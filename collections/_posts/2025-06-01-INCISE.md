The 2025 INCISE meeting has just come to a close, a whirlwind tour of international talks focused on submarine canyon processes and sediment transport along continental margins. Researchers presented new observations from moorings, repeat seafloor mapping, and sediment cores that are improving our understanding of how sediment is transported and preserved in canyon systems. A major theme was the increasing role of high-resolution multibeam mapping, which now resolves bedform-scale morphology in the deep ocean and enables repeat surveys to detect seabed change through time. These advances are providing a more detailed, process-based view of sediment transport and canyon evolution with some new accounting for the spatial extent of these processes. The meeting was an excellent opportunity to share results and connect with colleagues across oceanography, geology, and geophysics, and highlighted the progress being made in observing and understanding these dynamic environments.
