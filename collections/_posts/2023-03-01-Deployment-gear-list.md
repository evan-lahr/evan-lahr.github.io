Over the years, I’ve standardized a set of cruise prep spreadsheets to reduce last-minute surprises, prevent forgotten hardware, and ensure that instrument systems go from lab to seafloor efficiently. These lists break preparation into logical stages — instrument build-up, packing for deployment, and at-sea logistics — so that every bolt, connector, cable, and tool is accounted for before mobilization. They also make it easier to delegate tasks across team members while maintaining consistency between cruises.

## Why Structured Lists Matter

- Prevent missing critical hardware (fasteners, spares, specialty tools)  
- Separate lab assembly from shipboard packing tasks  
- Improve redundancy and spare-part tracking  
- Speed up deck operations during tight weather windows  
- Provide documentation for future cruises  

Good field science depends on repeatability — and that starts with preparation. Standardized deployment lists reduce cognitive load during busy mobilizations and help ensure that once offshore, the focus can remain on data collection rather than logistics.
