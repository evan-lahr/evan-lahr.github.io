This project explores how ocean-driven processes control sediment transport in submarine canyons along the northern Cascadia margin, with a focus on how canyon morphology influences the triggering and propagation of sediment gravity flows. Submarine canyons act as major conduits for sediment, nutrients, and organic matter from coastal rivers to the deep ocean, but the mechanisms that initiate downslope flows and determine their extent remain poorly understood. By studying two morphologically distinct canyons—Astoria and Quinault—the project aims to reveal how oceanic forcing, sediment supply, and canyon shape interact to drive sediment mobilization and deposition.

The research integrates multi-year benthic sensor deployments, seabed coring, and sediment transport modeling to quantify the conditions that generate sediment gravity flows. Key questions include where flows are initiated, whether flows can become self-sustaining through autosuspension, and how deposits from these events are preserved in the stratigraphic record. By combining time-series measurements with seabed sediment analysis, the project will link the dynamics of water and sediment movement in the canyon heads to the formation of turbidites and other sedimentary deposits at depth. This approach addresses long-standing gaps in understanding canyon activity during modern sea-level highstands and challenges the assumption that many canyons are currently “inactive.”

Scientifically, this project provides the first high-resolution, observationally constrained view of sediment transport dynamics through northern Cascadia canyons, improving our ability to interpret deep-sea stratigraphy and identify triggers of sediment gravity flows, including earthquakes and storms. Societally, the work informs coastal hazard assessment, submarine sediment dynamics, and ecosystem management, particularly for communities along the Cascadia margin, such as the Quinault Indian Nation. The project also provides extensive training for the next generation of ocean scientists, offering hands-on experience in fieldwork, sediment analysis, and modeling of complex ocean processes.
